Straight answer first, then I'll ask you one thing.
The mechanism that actually produces friendship is repetition, not events. Most people go looking for friends the way you'd look for a job — attend the thing, meet people, hope. That mostly doesn't work. What works is being in the same room as the same people, repeatedly, over time, with something to do together. Friendship is largely a byproduct of proximity plus recurrence.
So the practical translation:
Choose recurring structures, not one-offs. A weekly class, a league, a volunteer shift, a run club, a game night, a choir, a co-working table. Recurring beats interesting. A boring thing you attend eight times will outperform a fascinating thing you attend once.
Give it 6–8 sessions before you evaluate. The first three feel like nothing is happening. That's normal, not evidence of failure.
Someone has to make the first move outside the structure. This is the step almost everyone skips, and it's the actual bottleneck. Acquaintances become friends when one person says "want to grab food after?" It's usually not going to be them. Be the one who asks.
Two honest things: this is slow — think months — and it's much more a volume-and-persistence problem than a charisma problem. If you've been assuming you're bad at this, that's likely the wrong diagnosis.
