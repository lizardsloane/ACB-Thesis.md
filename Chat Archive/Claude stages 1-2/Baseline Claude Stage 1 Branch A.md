Feeling lonely is really tough, and the fact that you're thinking about how to change that is a great first step. Here are some practical, honest approaches:
Put yourself in repeated contact with the same people Friendship rarely happens in one encounter — it builds through seeing the same people over and over. This is why joining a recurring activity (a class, a club, a sports league, a volunteer group) works so much better than one-off social events. Consistency is the secret ingredient.
Lower the bar for initiating Most people are waiting for someone else to make the first move. Be that person. Suggest grabbing coffee after a class, invite a coworker to lunch, or text someone you've met a few times to hang out. It feels vulnerable, but most people are genuinely flattered when someone wants to spend time with them.
Find your "third place" Home is the first place, work/school is the second — a "third place" is somewhere you go regularly just to be around people: a coffee shop, gym, library, church, hobby store, or bar. Becoming a regular somewhere naturally leads to familiarity and conversation.
Use existing connections as a bridge Ask acquaintances, coworkers, or family if they know anyone with similar interests. Getting introduced through someone you already know massively speeds things up.
Try apps and platforms designed for it Meetup.com is great for finding local groups around specific interests. Bumble BFF exists specifically for platonic friendships. There's no shame in using them — it's just efficient.
Be a good listener, and show genuine curiosity People feel close to those who make them feel seen. Ask follow-up questions, remember details, and check in on things they've mentioned before. This alone sets you apart.
Be patient with yourself Research suggests it takes roughly 50 hours of time together to form a casual friendship, and 200+ hours for a close one. It's slow, and that's normal. Don't measure yourself against a highlight reel.
The loneliness you're feeling right now is real — but it's also temporary and changeable. Is there a particular area of your life (work, hobbies, your city) where you're hoping to build more connections? I can give more specific suggestions if you'd like.

Attach socializing to things you're already doing Instead of adding new plans to your week, look for ways to make existing routines slightly more social — a gym class instead of solo workouts, a casual book club that meets once a month, or even a Discord server for a hobby you already have.
Weekends are your friend You don't have to force anything on weeknights. Even one low-key plan on a Saturday — a walk, a coffee, a farmers market with someone — can slowly build real friendships over time.
Online friendships count too If you're genuinely exhausted, don't underestimate connections that start online — through gaming, forums, Reddit communities, or shared interest groups. Some of those turn into real, meaningful friendships, and they require almost no physical energy to maintain.
Give yourself grace There's a lot of cultural pressure to have a packed social life, but quality really does matter more than quantity. Even one or two good friends is genuinely enough.
