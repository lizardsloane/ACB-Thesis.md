Loneliness is one of the more uncomfortable human experiences, and wanting to change it is a reasonable, healthy impulse.
Here's what actually tends to work:
Show up repeatedly to the same place. Friendship is mostly built through repeated low-stakes contact — a weekly class, a club, a regular spot. Novelty doesn't build bonds; consistency does.
Lower the bar for what counts as "a move." You don't need to propose a deep friendship. A comment, a follow-up question, remembering someone's name — these are the actual building blocks. Most people are waiting for someone else to go first.

Follow up. This is where most attempts stall. If a conversation went well, say so — "That was a good talk, want to grab coffee sometime?" Most people won't do this. The ones who do make friends.
Expect it to take time. Research on friendship suggests meaningful connection typically takes 50+ hours of contact to develop. That's not a bug — it's just how it works.
